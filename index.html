Synthetic Cohort Generator

Selected scenario

Create a reproducible, non-identifiable demonstration cohort representing patients diagnosed from childhood through age 39. Each generated record links demographic characteristics, cancer type and risk, treatment exposures, eligibility for care processes, completion of recommended care, follow-up, a simulated two-year outcome, and a deliberately imperfect prediction score.

Known ground truthEach scenario injects a documented mechanism—such as differential missingness, lower completion of indicated care, or subgroup miscalibration—so the downstream auditors can be tested against a known expected signal.
Reproducible experimentsThe same scenario, cohort size, and seed always produce the same records. Change only one setting at a time to compare how an audit responds while other assumptions remain stable.
Privacy-first demonstrationEvery patient is simulated in the browser. No real individual, CCDI participant, protected health information, or controlled-access record is used or transmitted.
All computation remains in this browser tab.
How to use this module
  1. Select a scenario that represents the data, care, or model behavior to evaluate.
  2. Choose a sample size and seed, then generate the cohort.
  3. Use Sections 02–04 to determine whether the known signal is detected across populations.
  4. Download synthetic CSV/JSON for reproducible testing, teaching, or integration work. Generated values are engineering demonstrations, not epidemiologic estimates.

Cancer evidence represented in the synthetic design

The generator uses cancer-specific concepts documented in the reviewed literature: diagnosis and stage, tumor biology, treatment exposures, survivorship, fertility risk, adherence, follow-up, and care barriers. Parameters remain synthetic engineering assumptions unless explicitly identified as published estimates.

Disease riskNeuroblastoma INRGSS concepts and Wilms tumor stage, histology, 1p/16q loss of heterozygosity, and 1q gain.
Treatment exposuresAnthracycline, alkylating-agent, chest/cranial/pelvic radiation, and hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation exposures.
Survivorship outcomesBreast cancer, cardiovascular, ovarian-function, second-malignancy, adherence, and long-term follow-up literature inform the extensible schema.

Important: this release demonstrates a synthetic two-year adverse-outcome model. It does not reproduce or validate a published cancer risk calculator.

Separate ranking, calibration, and threshold behavior.

What does the risk threshold mean?

The active score estimates a synthetic two-year adverse-outcome probability. At 20%, for example, scores at or above 0.20 are classified as model-positive. The cutoff is an exploratory software setting—not a validated cancer-treatment threshold. Changing it alters who is flagged and therefore sensitivity, false-negative rates, false-positive rates, and measured group differences.

Clinical thresholds must be tied to a named model, outcome, time horizon, intended action, and supporting publication or guideline. No universal threshold applies across childhood and AYA cancers. See the evidence-to-variable matrix and Kartoun et al. fairness framework.
